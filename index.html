Performance Training

Goal: Build total body strength, focusing on power transfer from the ground up.

Frequency: 3-4 days/week

Freshmen / JV Focus

Master form with bodyweight or light resistance. Do not push heavy weights until mechanics are perfect.

Varsity Focus (Mass & Power)

Progressive overload. During Phases 2 & 3, focus on hypertrophy (8-12 reps) if you need to add mass, then transition to lower rep power sets.

Muscle Gain Protocol (Hypertrophy Block)

For players needing to add physical mass (especially Varsity): During the first 6-8 weeks of lifting, utilize a hypertrophy rep range on major compound lifts. This means 3 to 4 sets of 8 to 12 repetitions, leaving 1-2 reps in the tank (RPE 8). Combine this with the 'Game Plan: Growth' in the Nutrition tab.

Arm Care & Maintenance

Protecting the arm is the most critical aspect of the off-season. Following Jaeger, do this routine before every throwing session, and 3–5 days a week when you are not throwing. J-Bands first.

Rotator Cuff Health

External & Internal Rotation

External Rotation: 1 set of 25 (J-Band #7).

Clip at hip height, elbow on hip. Off hand on outside of elbow. Rotate out.

Internal Rotation: 1 set of 25 (J-Band #6).

Clip at hip height, elbow on hip. Off hand under armpit. Rotate toward opposite hip.

Coach's Note: Pitchers often have strong internal rotation but weak external rotation. Focus on slow, controlled eccentric (lowering) movements on external rotation.

Scapular Stability

  • Band Pull-Aparts (2x15)
  • Face Pulls (2x15)
  • Scapular Push-ups (2x15)
  • Sleeper Stretches (3x30 sec)
  • Y/T/W/I Raises (2x10, hold 2 sec, 1-3 lbs or no weight)

Band Routine: Step by Step

Every exercise in your arm care routine, drawn out. Do it before every throwing session, and 3–5 days a week when you are not throwing. J-Bands first, then shoulder blade work.

Start Finish Movement Band Ball

J-Band Routine: all 11 exercises (Jaeger)

Before every throwing session, or 3–5 days a week when you are not throwing. 1 set of 25 each. Never let the clip line up with your face or head, and never stretch the band more than 1–2 feet. Walk closer to the anchor to make it easier. Work to fatigue, not failure.

1. Over-the-Head Forearm Extensions
1 × 25
  • Lunge stance, front knee over front heel
  • Elbows stay still by your ears
  • Extend forearms and palms forward
2. Side Extensions
30-sec stretch holds
  • Lunge stance, a little slack in the band
  • Lengthen your side, do not round it
  • Reach from back hip through fingertips, breathe
3. Diagonal Extensions
30-sec stretch holds
  • Clip at mid-back height, behind you
  • Lunge stance, front knee over heel
  • Reach diagonally from back hip through fingertips, breathe
4. Forward Flies
1 × 25
  • Clip at chest height, behind you
  • Slight bend in the elbows
  • Bring palms toward each other
5. Reverse Flies
1 × 25
  • Face the clip
  • Palms move away from each other
  • Knees bent, chin over toes, head still
6. Internal Rotation
1 × 25
  • Clip at hip height, elbow on your hip, bent 90°
  • Off hand under your armpit, shoulders level
  • Rotate toward your opposite hip
7. External Rotation
1 × 25
  • Clip at hip height, arm bent 90°
  • Off hand on the outside of your elbow
  • Rotate out, shoulders level
8. Elevated Internal Rotation
1 × 25
  • Clip at shoulder height
  • Elbow at shoulder height, in line with the clip
  • Rotate forward and down, elbow stays put
9. Elevated External Rotation
1 × 25
  • Clip at shoulder height
  • Same as #8, opposite direction
  • Rotate up and back, elbow stays put
10. Reverse Throwing
1 × 25
  • Clip at waist height, glove shoulder facing it
  • Throwing motion in reverse, same arm slot
  • Make a complete arm circle
11. Forward Throwing Motion
1 × 25
  • Clip at waist height, fingers through the wrist cuff
  • Normal throwing mechanics, tubing in your arm slot
  • Full arm circle, loose and relaxed

Shoulder Blade Strength & Mobility

Same schedule as the J-Bands: right after them, before every throwing session and 3–5 days a week when you are not throwing.

Band Pull-Aparts
2 × 15
  • Arms straight in front at chest height
  • Pull the band to your chest
  • Squeeze shoulder blades, no shrugging
Face Pulls
2 × 15
  • Band anchored at head height
  • Pull toward your forehead, elbows high
  • Finish with hands by your ears
Scapular Push-ups
2 × 15
  • High plank, arms locked straight
  • Let your chest sink a little
  • Push the floor away, upper back rounds
Y / T / W / I Raises
2 × 10 each · hold 2 sec
  • Lie face down or bend at the hips
  • 1–3 lb or no weight, thumbs up for Y and T
  • Hold the top for 2 seconds, lower slowly
Sleeper Stretch
3 × 30 sec
  • Lie on your throwing side
  • Elbow at shoulder height, bent 90°
  • Other hand eases wrist down, gentle only

Plyo Ball Drills: Step by Step

Phases 2 & 3 only, after arm care and a full warm-up. These are throwing drills against a wall at 60–70% effort. They build clean mechanics, not max velocity.

Read first: Stop right away if anything hurts. Never throw a plyo ball at full effort. Heavier balls (1–2 lb) are for reverse throws only, and only for Varsity.
Freshmen / JV Light balls only (3.5–7 oz), including reverse throws. Start with pivot pickoffs and roll-ins, and use the low end of each rep range.
Varsity Full rep ranges. Drills with 9 oz or lighter balls; 1–2 lb only for reverse throws.
Every session J-Bands and arm care first. 60–70% effort on every throw. Quality over quantity.
Start Finish Movement Band Ball
Reverse Throws

Wall behind you

1–2 × 10
  • Stand with your back to the wall
  • Rock forward, then swing the ball back hard into the wall
  • Thumb up, chest tall, front knee firm

Varsity: 1–2 lb ball. Freshmen/JV: light ball. Never full effort.

Pivot Pickoffs

Facing sideways to the wall

1–2 × 8
  • Start square, ball in glove
  • Pivot on your back foot toward the wall
  • Throw with a clean arm path, smooth finish

Light ball. Focus on timing, not speed.

Roll-ins

Feet set in a stride

1–2 × 8
  • Set your feet in your stride position
  • Turn the hips in, let the arm come through
  • Feel the hips lead the shoulders

Light ball. Mechanics only.

Rockers

Wide stance

1–2 × 8
  • Wide stance, front foot slightly closed
  • Rock back, then rock through and throw
  • Shift weight forward, not up

Light ball. Add a few weeks into the program.

Step-Behinds

Back to the target at start

6 throws
  • Start with your back toward the wall
  • Cross the back foot behind the front foot
  • Drive the hips at the wall, front shoulder stays closed

2 throws each with a 7 oz, 5 oz, then 3.5 oz ball.

Walking Torques

A few steps from the wall

16 throws
  • Walk in with a couple of steps
  • Stride toward the wall, back leg long
  • Chest up as the torso turns

8 throws with a 7 oz ball, then 8 with a 5 oz ball.

Throwing Build-Up Progression

IMPORTANT: Never throw through sharp pain. Soreness is okay; pain is not.
1

Weeks 1-4: Rest

NO THROWING

Focus entirely on physical recovery, mobility, and the beginning of the lifting program.

2

Weeks 5-8: Light Catch

  • Frequency: 2 days a week
  • Distance: 60-90 feet max
  • Intensity: 50%
  • Focus on mechanics, arm path, and fluid motion.
3

Weeks 9-12: Long Toss Ramp Up

  • Frequency: 3 days a week
  • Begin long toss program (90ft → 120ft → 150ft).
  • Incorporate "pull downs" on the way back in.
Freshmen: Cap distance at 120-150 feet. Varsity: Push to 200+ feet if arm strength allows.
4

Weeks 13-16: Mound Work

Transition from flat ground to the mound.

Wk 13: Flat ground bullpens (75%), FB command.
Wk 14: 1-2 light mound pens (20-25 pitches, FB only).
Wk 15: 2 pens. Intro changeups. 85% intensity (30-35 pitches).
Wk 16: 2 pens. Intro breaking balls (Varsity/approved JV). Simulate game situations (40-45 pitches).

Phase 1 (Wks 1-4): Rest

NO THROWING

Phase 2 (Wks 5-8): Light Catch

2x/week, 60-90ft max, 50% intensity.

Phase 3 (Wks 9-12): Long Toss

3x/week. Build distance. Freshmen cap 150ft, Varsity push 200+ft.

Phase 4 (Wks 13-16): Bullpens

Transition to mound. Build pitch count (20 -> 45). Intro off-speed gradually.

Nutrition Guide

Fueling the body is essential for recovery, adding lean muscle mass, and increasing velocity on the mound.

Hydration

Half body weight in oz daily. Add electrolytes on training days.

Protein

0.8-1.2g per lb of body weight. Chicken, turkey, lean beef, fish, eggs, greek yogurt, cottage cheese, whey protein, lentils.

Carbs

Primary energy. Sweet/white potatoes, brown/white rice, oatmeal, quinoa, whole-wheat pasta/bread, beans, bananas, berries.

Fats

Hormone & joint health. Avocados, mixed nuts, chia/flax seeds, olive oil, peanut butter, salmon, cheese.

Game Plan: Growth (Mass Gain)

Add Mass

Essential for increasing velocity. Mass = Gas.

  • Must be in a caloric surplus (+300-500 calories above maintenance daily).
  • High Protein: Aim for 1.0 - 1.2g of protein per lb of body weight to build muscle.
  • Add a high-calorie protein shake (milk, oats, peanut butter, whey) daily.
  • Eat frequently: 3 large main meals + 2-3 substantial snacks. Never skip breakfast.

Game Plan: Maintenance

Lean Out
  • Focus on lean proteins and high vegetable intake.
  • Limit processed sugars and refined carbohydrates.
  • Maintain a slight caloric deficit or eat at maintenance level.

Mental Game & Sleep

Your arm gets you to the mound. Your mind decides what happens once you're there. Train it like you train your body: a few minutes, every day.

Breathe: 5 minutes a day

Meditation is reps for your focus. Your mind will wander. Each time you notice and come back to your breath, that's one rep, and it's the same skill you use to come back to the next pitch after a bad one.

Ready

 

How to do it

  1. 1Same time every day: after school, after your lift, or in bed at night.
  2. 2Sit tall or lie down. Phone on Do Not Disturb. Close your eyes.
  3. 3Breathe in through your nose, low into your belly. Follow the timer.
  4. 4Mind wandered? No big deal. Notice it, then come back to the breath. That's the rep.

On the mound

Box breathing (in 4, hold 4, out 4, hold 4) is for daily practice and before you warm up. The reset sigh (two breaths in through the nose, one long breath out) takes about 10 seconds. Use it on the back of the mound between pitches.

Don't ride the wave

Strikeout or home run, you look the same. Same tempo, same body language, same routine. Pitchers who ride every high and low lose control of the inning. Pitchers who stay even get the next out.

Your pitch-to-pitch reset

Step 1

Release

Let the last pitch go with a physical action: turn your back to the plate, grab the rosin, fix your cap. Pick one and use it every time.

Step 2

Reset

One reset sigh: two breaths in through the nose, one long breath out. Shoulders drop. Heart rate comes down.

Step 3

Refocus

Look at your focal point (foul pole, scoreboard, a spot on the fence) and say your cue word. Get the sign. Commit to one pitch.

Know your light

  • Green: locked in, breathing slow, trusting your stuff. Keep going.
  • Yellow: rushing, overthrowing, glancing at the ump or the dugout. Slow down and do your full reset.
  • Red: angry, tight, can't stop thinking about the last pitch. Step off the rubber. Two reset sighs. Then one pitch at a time.

The traffic-light idea comes from sports psychologist Ken Ravizza's work with baseball players.

Say what to do, not what to avoid

Instead ofSay
Don't walk himHit the glove
Don't leave it upDown and through
I always blow it hereI've trained for this
That ump is killing meNext pitch

Control the controllables: effort, attitude, body language, preparation, the next pitch. Not yours to control: the ump, the weather, errors behind you, the last pitch.

See it first: visualization

Picture pitches clearly enough, with what you see, feel and hear, and you're practicing your delivery without using up any of your arm. Most elite athletes do it. It takes 5 minutes, and lying in bed at night is a great time.

  1. 1Get calm. Five slow breaths, eyes closed.
  2. 2See it through your own eyes. The mound under your feet, the catcher, the target in his glove.
  3. 3Feel it. Seams on your fingers, weight loading into your back leg, your arm working through the motion.
  4. 4Hear it. The crowd, your catcher's voice, the pop of the mitt.
  5. 5Throw 10 pitches in real time. Each one hits the glove. Use your real delivery speed, not slow motion.
  6. 6Add trouble. An error puts a runner on second. See yourself do your release, reset and refocus, then make your pitch.

When to use it

  • Phases 1–2 (no or light throwing): picture clean mechanics 3 times a week.
  • Phase 3: picture your long toss and pull-downs the night before.
  • Phase 4: picture the whole bullpen the night before, pitch by pitch.
  • Game day: picture your first three batters before you warm up.

Make it real. Wear your spikes or hold a ball while you do it. The more it feels like the mound, the better it carries over.

Sleep: the free performance boost

Sleep is when the work you did today turns into results. Short sleep means slower recovery, slower reactions and a higher chance of getting hurt.

8–10

hours a night

What sleep doctors recommend for teens 13–18 (American Academy of Sleep Medicine).

1.7×

more likely to get hurt

Middle and high school athletes who slept under 8 hours, compared with those who slept more (Milewski et al., 2014).

+9%

shooting accuracy

Stanford basketball players who spent 10 hours in bed each night shot better and sprinted faster (Mah et al., 2011).

Your body

Deep sleep is when your body releases most of its growth hormone. That's when muscle from your lifts gets rebuilt and your arm recovers from throwing.

Your mind

Sleep locks in the mechanics you practiced that day. It also controls mood, focus and reaction time. Tired pitchers lose command and get frustrated faster.

Your arm

A tired body moves worse, and bad mechanics load the shoulder and elbow. Sleep is part of your arm care.

Sleep playbook

  • Same bedtime and wake time every day, weekends within an hour.
  • Screens off 30–60 minutes before bed. Charge your phone outside the bedroom if you can.
  • Cool, dark, quiet room.
  • No caffeine or energy drinks after lunch.
  • Short night? A 20–30 minute nap before 4 pm helps. Longer naps can wreck your night.
  • Can't fall asleep? Do 5 minutes of box breathing or a visualization in bed.
